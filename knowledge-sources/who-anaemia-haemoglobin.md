# Anaemia and Haemoglobin Reference Thresholds (WHO)

Haemoglobin (Hb) carries oxygen in red blood cells. Anaemia is diagnosed below
these haemoglobin concentrations (at sea level), per the World Health Organization:

- Children 6–59 months: below 11.0 g/dL
- Children 5–11 years: below 11.5 g/dL
- Children 12–14 years and non-pregnant women (15+): below 12.0 g/dL
- Pregnant women: below 11.0 g/dL
- Men (15+): below 13.0 g/dL

Severity grading (non-pregnant adults): mild 11.0–11.9 g/dL (women) / 11.0–12.9 g/dL
(men); moderate 8.0–10.9 g/dL; severe below 8.0 g/dL.

A low Hb with a low MCV (microcytic red cells) most commonly indicates iron
deficiency; a ferritin test helps confirm iron stores.

Source: World Health Organization — Haemoglobin concentrations for the diagnosis of
anaemia and assessment of severity (VMNIS, 2011). Educational reference only; not a diagnosis.
